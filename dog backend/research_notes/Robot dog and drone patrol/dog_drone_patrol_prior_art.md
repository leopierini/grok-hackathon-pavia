# Robot Dog + Drone Collaborative Security Patrol: Prior Art, Market and Gaps (as of Nov 2026)

## Q1. Commercial products and deployments pairing quadrupeds and drones for security and inspection

### Takeaway
"Drone spots, robot dog investigates" already exists commercially. Asylon Robotics (US) sells exactly this as a managed service: Guardian drones plus Spot-based DroneDog robots, with a human remote operations center (RSOC) as the dispatcher. Percepto and DroneDeploy/Rocos showed air-to-ground handoff for industrial inspection as early as 2020-2021, and in China DEEP Robotics runs "robot dog + drone" substation and wind-farm inspections. The basic concept is therefore not novel. What remains thin is automated, LLM/VLM-driven reasoning and dispatch between the two. Today a human operator or a fixed rule still makes that decision.

### Inferences
- Novelty is **low** for the concept "drone spots, robot dog investigates." Asylon sells it, with a $17M Securitas contract and USAF contracts, and Percepto and DroneDeploy demonstrated it for inspection in 2020-2021.
- Novelty is **moderate** for an *AI dispatcher*: an LLM that triages drone detections, chooses which asset to send, writes the scene description and drafts the guard alert. In every commercial system found, a human RSOC or a fixed alarm rule makes that call. Spot's AIVI/Gemini provides VLM description on the ground robot alone, not cross-asset orchestration.
- Market structure suggests a hackathon pitch should be framed as a vendor-agnostic "AI security operations officer" software layer above existing hardware, not as a new robot. In Europe, no Asylon-equivalent managed dog+drone service was found.

### Gaps
- Nightingale Security, Sunflower Labs (Bee/Beehive), Skydio Dock security integrations, ANYbotics (ANYmal is mainly industrial inspection) and Hexagon were not verified for any dog+drone pairing in this round of searches.
- Securitas deal signing date and Asylon unit pricing were not found. The Forbes original returned 403.
- No source confirmed a commercial Unitree quadruped with a drone carried on its back.
- No European (EU/Italy) commercial dog+drone security deployment was found.

## Q2. Research on UAV–UGV / UAV–legged cooperation and LLM/VLM coordination of air-ground teams

### Takeaway
Air-ground cooperation for exploration and mapping is a mature research area (CERBERUS/DARPA SubT 2019-2021, marsupial systems 2022-2025). LLM-based heterogeneous multi-robot planning appeared in 2023-2025: SMART-LLM, COHERENT (ICRA 2025, which includes quadrotors and robot dogs), and Cladera et al. 2025 (an LLM planner for a UAV+UGV team on natural-language missions). VLM video anomaly detection (LAVAD, Cerberus 2025) is also active. No paper found closes the full **security** loop: drone VLM anomaly detection, then an LLM threat assessment, then quadruped dispatch, then a generated incident report.

### Cited Findings
- **Collaborative navigation for flying and walking robots** (ETH, ~2016): a hexacopter maps the area, and the quadruped plans and localizes against the aerial map. This is the origin of "aerial map for ground navigation." — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/305808109_Collaborative_Navigation_for_Flying_and_Walking_Robots)
- **CERBERUS** (DARPA SubT, JFR 2021): ANYmal quadrupeds plus aerial scouts with a unified exploration planner. — [Oxford PDF](https://robots.ox.ac.uk/~mfallon/publications/2021JFR_tranzatto.pdf). **Autonomous Teamed Exploration with Legged and Aerial Robots** (arXiv 2111.06482): a Hagerbach mine trial with the legged robot running 37 min and the aerial robot 8 min, using an open-source graph-based planner. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.06482)
- **Stronger Together: Air-Ground Robotic Collaboration Using Semantics** (UPenn, arXiv 2206.14289, 2022): semantic maps shared between UAV and UGV. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.14289)
- **Marsupial walking-and-flying deployment** (arXiv 2205.05477, 2022), and **task-driven map compression for a marsupial ground-aerial team** (ANYmal-D + RMF-Owl, arXiv 2509.07655, Sep 2025) with onboard co-localization, map and target sharing. — [arXiv 2205.05477](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.05477); [arXiv 2509.07655](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.07655v1)
- **Hierarchical graph-based terrain-aware navigation for multimodal ground-aerial exploration** (arXiv 2505.14859, 2025): the ground robot chooses where to deploy the aerial robot. The authors note that prior systems "mostly rely on pre-defined scenarios to identify when the aerial robot should be deployed." This is a decision gap an LLM could address. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.14859)
- **UAV-legged robot synergy for cooperative exploration and planning** (Li, Xu, Ding, Yang; IEEE, Nov 2024; metadata only). — via search results; content not verified
- **Survey: Multi-robot cooperative exploration** (arXiv 2503.07278, 2025). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.07278)
- **LLM, air-ground specific: Cladera, Ravichandran, Hughes et al., "Air-Ground Collaboration for Language-Specified Missions in Unknown Environments"** (arXiv 2505.09108, May 2025, UPenn). A UAV and a UGV carry out natural-language missions. An LLM planner reasons over semantic-metric maps that the two robots share opportunistically, and it adapts when the mission specification changes. Tested in urban and rural settings. **This is the closest academic prior art to an "LLM orchestrates drone+ground robot" system.** — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09108)
- **COHERENT** (Liu et al., arXiv 2409.15146, ICRA 2025): an LLM task planner for heterogeneous teams of **quadrotors, robot dogs and robotic arms**. A central assigner LLM decomposes the task and each robot runs an executor LLM in a proposal-execution-feedback-adjustment loop. The benchmark has 100 tasks across 5 scenes, it includes real-world tests, and the code is public. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.15146v3)
- **SMART-LLM** (Kannan et al., arXiv 2309.10062, 2023/IROS 2024): few-shot LLM task decomposition, coalition formation and allocation across robots with different skills. — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2309.10062)
- **LLMs for Multi-Robot Systems: A Survey** (arXiv 2502.03814, Feb 2025). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.03814)
- **Heterogeneous Robot Collaboration in Unstructured Environments with Grounded Generative Intelligence** (arXiv 2510.26915, Oct 2025). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26915) (content not reviewed in detail)
- **IndoorR2X: Indoor Robot-to-Everything Coordination with LLM-Driven Planning** (arXiv 2603.20182, Mar 2026). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.20182)
- **UAV Vision-and-Language Navigation roadmap** (arXiv 2604.13654, Apr 2026) and **UAVs meet embodied intelligence / physical-digital AI agents** (arXiv 2609.18326, Sep 2026). — [arXiv 2604.13654](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.13654); [arXiv 2609.18326](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.18326)
- **VLM video anomaly detection:** Cerberus (arXiv 2510.16290, Oct 2025) is a real-time cascade in which a lightweight filter feeds VLM reasoning, combined with motion-mask prompting and rule-based deviation detection. LAVAD (CVPR 2024) is a training-free pipeline: the VLM captions frames and an LLM scores anomalies, which is competitive on UCF-Crime. — [arXiv 2510.16290](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.16290)
- **Drone-specific anomaly detection difficulty:** repeated patrol passes never align exactly, because wind and GPS error shift the viewpoint and lighting changes, so naive pixel differencing fails. PLaMo 2.1-VL uses a two-image difference model. — [PLaMo 2.1-VL report, arXiv 2604.19324](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.19324); an older baseline is in [IEEE 8616425, drone surveillance anomaly detection](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/8616425/)
- Industry architecture guidance (2026): high-precision detection on the edge, with a cloud VLM re-scoring borderline events. — [Fora Soft, 2026](https://www.forasoft.com/blog/article/automated-anomaly-detection-security-cameras)

### Inferences
- A hackathon system that (1) runs VLM anomaly triage on drone frames, (2) has an LLM decide whether to send the dog, ask the drone to loiter, or escalate to a guard, (3) passes target coordinates from air to ground, and (4) writes an alert in Italian or English would combine Cladera 2025, COHERENT and LAVAD-style VAD into a **security-specific closed loop**. That combination was not found as a published system or product.
- Tested-and-proven parts the team can cite: air-to-ground map and target sharing (2509.07655, Stronger Together), and LLM heterogeneous task allocation (COHERENT). Weak parts to acknowledge: VLM latency and hallucination, and how often the LLM's dispatch decisions are reliable.

### Gaps
- No benchmark or dataset was found for "air-ground security incident response". This is an opportunity for the team to define a small one.
- Full texts of 2510.26915, the IEEE 2024 UAV-legged synergy paper, and "air-ground VLN via shared BEV maps" (cited elsewhere as arXiv 2609.03483) were not verified.
- No quantitative study was found comparing LLM-based and rule-based dispatch in security operations.

## Q3. Typical industrial use case (~55,000 m² site; Riello) and buyer pain

### Takeaway
Riello's Legnago (Verona) complex is consistent with a site of roughly 55-60k m². No public information was found on how Riello secures it, so the team should treat CCTV, guards and alarms as the assumed baseline. The buyer pains that robot vendors cite (coverage, guard cost, response time, false alarms) are real but backed mostly by vendor data. The strongest evidence is Asylon/Bayer's reduced vehicle patrols and costs, and DEEP Robotics' inspection-time savings.

### Cited Findings
- Riello's own document: Legnago HQ is 3,000 m² plus a 700 m² training centre, and the S. Pietro di Legnago production site is 22,000 m². A secondary source gives the burner centre as about 22,000 m² "out of a total of 60,000 m²" (unconfirmed). — [Riello/Este presentation PDF](https://www.storicoeventi.este.it/images/file-pdf/Presentazione%20Riello_Este_205.pdf)
- Ownership: Ariston Group agreed to acquire Riello from Carrier, including the Legnago and Volpago plants, so Riello is returning to Italian ownership. — [ItaliaOggi](https://www.italiaoggi.it/marketing-e-media/marketing/ariston-group-acquisisce-riello-che-torna-in-mani-italiane-elmbtrho); [MIMIT](https://www.mimit.gov.it/it/notizie-stampa/riello-mimit-al-via-monitoraggio-verso-miglior-soluzione-produttiva-e-occupazionale)
- Italian private-security sector: about 1,500 companies and almost €5B turnover. ENAC and the security associations ran a joint technical table on drones. Mondialpol uses zoom-camera drones to support its guards. A CONSIP public tender "for the first time institutionalised" drones in surveillance services. — [Dronezine](https://www.dronezine.it/?p=26105); [Dronezine, guards + drone](https://www.dronezine.it/?p=455385)
- Buyer evidence: Bayer supplemented overnight human patrols with DroneDog, cut daily vehicle patrols and reduced security operating costs, then expanded to more sites. — [DirectIndustry, Apr 2026](https://emag.directindustry.com/2026/04/24/high-tech-robotic-dogs-now-on-patrol-guarding-valuable-corn-crops-dronedog/). GXO (logistics) deployed DroneDog plus aerial drones, and one site logged more than 600 patrol miles. — [GXO](https://investors.gxo.com/news-releases/news-release-details/gxo-enhances-security-and-safety-high-tech-dronedog-and-aerial)
- Inspection ROI (company claims): DEEP Robotics substation inspection takes under 35 minutes versus about 2 hours manually, with about 70% lower O&M cost. — [Newsfile](https://www.newsfilecorp.com/release/273102). Shanghai patrols claim "millions of yuan" in annual savings. — [Shanghai Daily](https://www.citynewsservice.cn/articles/shanghaidaily/news/shanghai-deploys-robot-dogs-drones-for-city-patrols-dmjb0gen)
- False alarms and response time: published figures vary widely. One SEC paper example gives a 3% false-alarm rate at unstaffed facilities, and a vendor guide uses 76% as an illustration. A vendor case cites a roughly 30-minute response against a "standard 3-5 minute" protocol. Re-tuning sensors cut false alarms by 80% at one German site. Intrusions are stopped only if barrier delay exceeds response-force travel time. — [Security Executive Council](https://www.securityexecutivecouncil.com/download/561); [Davantis guide](https://www.davantis.com/wp-content/uploads/perimeter-security-false-alarms-guide.pdf); [FFT Security](https://www.fftsecurity.com/the-impact-of-unreliable-security-systems-on-emergency-response-times-when-minutes-matter/); [SecurityWorldMarket](https://www.securityworldmarket.com/int/Newsarchive/complete-perimeter-protection-safeguards-rurtalwerkstatten-in-germany)

### Inferences
- On a ~55,000 m² site (roughly 235 m × 235 m, about 1 km of perimeter), a guard on foot needs several minutes to reach a far corner. A docked drone can be overhead in under a minute, and a quadruped at about 1.5 m/s covers 200 m in roughly 2-3 minutes. So "drone verifies fast, dog follows up close" is a credible fit. This is a back-of-envelope estimate, not sourced.
- The pain a hackathon judge from a manufacturer will recognize most is **alarm verification**: cutting nuisance call-outs and giving guards an AI-written, evidence-backed description before anyone walks out. A VLM/LLM layer addresses this directly. Patrol coverage at night and on weekends is the second pain.
- Industrial inspection (thermal hot spots, leaks, gauges) is a co-benefit that lets one fleet pay for both security and inspection. Percepto, DEEP Robotics and Spot's Orbit all sell on this dual use.

### Gaps
- No public information was found on Riello's actual security setup, its guard provider, or the exact 55,000 m² figure. The team should ask the Track D proposer directly.
- No independent (non-vendor) study of robot+drone patrol ROI or false-alarm reduction was found.

## Q4. What is still missing or unsolved; EU and Italy regulatory limits; market overview

### Takeaway
Open problems: autonomous (not human) cross-asset decision-making, reliable drone-based anomaly detection across repeated passes, trust and hallucination in LLM triage, cybersecurity of robot fleets, and EU/Italian drone rules. In the EU, an automated drone-in-a-box security patrol over a factory falls into EASA's **specific category** (an operational authorisation from ENAC via SORA, PDRA or STS). In Italy, private surveillance on behalf of third parties also raises licensing and GDPR issues. Markets are large and growing: security robots cluster around $15-19B in 2025 at an 11-17% CAGR, and drone-in-a-box around $1.4-1.5B in 2025, reaching about $7B by 2034.

### Cited Findings
**Regulation (EU / Italy)**
- EASA: operations outside open-category limits, for example BVLOS, fall into the specific category and need an operational authorisation from the national authority (ENAC in Italy). Exceptions are standard-scenario (STS) declarations and LUC holders. Remote ID has been mandatory in the specific category since 1 January 2024. — [EASA, Specific category](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/domains/drones-air-mobility/operating-drone/specific-category-civil-drones)
- STS-01 is VLOS over a controlled ground area and may be in populated areas. STS-02 is BVLOS up to 1 km (2 km with an airspace observer) over a controlled ground area in a sparsely populated environment. PDRA-S01/S02 mirror these for drones without the C5/C6 class mark. BVLOS over populated areas or assemblies of people was addressed in NPA 2020-07 (RMT.0730). — [EASA specific-category slides](https://www.easa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/dfu/EU_UAS_Regulation_specific-category.pdf); [EASA NPA 2020-07](https://www.easa.europa.eu/document-library/notices-of-proposed-amendment/npa-2020-07)

A drone-in-a-box operated 24/7 at the Torrevaldaliga Nord power plant (Enel, Civitavecchia). — [Agri-TechE](https://www.agri-tech-e.co.uk/member-post/uk-based-drone-ag-gains-unique-enac-bvlos-authorisation-for-autonomous-crop-trial-monitoring-in-italy/); [Drones World Mag](https://www.dronesworldmag.com/telespazio-obtains-authorisation-from-enac-as-a-drone-operator-for-bvlos-flight-scenarios/); [Wikipedia, Drone in a Box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drone_in_a_Box)

### Inferences
- **Open gaps a hackathon team can credibly claim:**
  1. Autonomous **cross-asset reasoning and dispatch**: an LLM that decides whether to send the dog, keep the drone loitering or alert a guard. Today a human RSOC or rules do this (Asylon, Tyndall).
  2. **Evidence-grounded alert writing**: a structured, auditable incident report with an image crop, location, a confidence score and a recommended action, aimed at fewer nuisance call-outs.
  3. **Air-to-ground target handoff in site coordinates** using the drone's geo-referenced detection as the dog's navigation goal (research exists; not productized with an LLM).
  4. **An EU-first, privacy-by-design posture**: on-prem or edge VLM, no foreign cloud, GDPR-aware face blurring, and a human-in-the-loop approval step for each dispatch, which also fits ENAC's specific-category expectations.
  5. **A security benchmark or simulation** for air-ground incident response, since none was found.
- Pitch risk: judges may know Asylon or Spot AIVI. Position the work as "the AI dispatcher/SOC copilot layer" and acknowledge Asylon as validation that buyers pay for dog+drone patrols (the $17M Securitas contract).
- In Italy, a realistic near-term deployment uses the drone over the owner's own fenced industrial premises (a controlled ground area) under STS/PDRA or SORA, with a remote pilot in the loop. That makes the "AI recommends, human approves" design not just safer but regulation-aligned.

### Gaps
- The exact current ENAC procedure for drone-in-a-box security over private industrial sites, and the current Italian licensing rules (TULPS/DM 269/2010) for security institutes using drones, were not verified from primary sources.
- The impact of the EU AI Act on AI-based surveillance and dispatch (e.g., high-risk classification, biometric limits) was not researched in this round.
- No security-segment share of the drone-in-a-box market was found. Market-report figures are unverified publisher estimates with inconsistent scopes.
